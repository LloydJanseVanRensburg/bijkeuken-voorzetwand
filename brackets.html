How the brackets hold the frame

Every timber-to-timber joint uses the same 90° angle bracket (hoekanker 70×70×55, 2.5 mm, galvanised) with 5×35 anchor screws (ankerschroeven), 4 per leg. This page shows where every bracket goes and how to fit each type of joint.

Three rules

Before the first bracket

  1. Side faces onlyBrackets go on the 94 mm faces that point along the wall, at the sides of a stud. Never on the front, where the OSB goes, or the back.
  2. Left 10 mm back, right 10 mm forwardA stud often has a bracket on both sides at the same height. The 35 mm screws from each side would meet in the middle of the 44 mm stud. So shift every bracket on a stud's left face 10 mm toward the wall, and every bracket on its right face 10 mm toward the room. The screws then pass each other.
  3. Tight in the cornerPush the bend of the bracket hard into the corner between the two pieces before screwing. Use the round holes, 4 per leg. Drive the screws until the head touches the steel, no further.

Joint 1 · Build journey step 4

Stud foot

  1. Stand the stud on its mark on the floor plate.
  2. Check it's plumb with the waterpas on two faces: the side and the front. Have someone hold it, or clamp it to a scrap.
  3. Push the bracket into the corner on the right face, 10 mm toward the room. Put 1 screw in the plate leg and 1 in the stud leg.
  4. Check plumb again. Then add the other 6 screws.
  5. Do the left face the same way, 10 mm toward the wall.
Joint 2

Stud top

  1. The top plate is already fixed to the ceiling (step 3).
  2. Set the stud on its foot mark and swing the top in under its mark on the top plate. Tap it in with a hammer and a block if it's tight.
  3. Check plumb. Bracket in the top corner, 10 mm forward on the right face and 10 mm back on the left.
  4. Screw up into the plate first, then into the stud.
Joint 3 · Build journey step 6

Noggins: the bracket is a shelf

The brackets go on first and the noggin is dropped onto them, so you never have to hold a noggin and screw at the same time.

  • Row 1 at 1180 skips the pillar bay and the open bay. Row 2 at 1811 runs in every bay, including N3 over the pillar and N4 over the open bay.
  • N3's brackets hang down to 1741, just above the pillar top at 1735. Check they clear it. If one touches, file or bend the corner a little, or fit that bracket on top of the noggin instead.

Joint 4 · Build journey step 5

Open bay: header and short studs

  1. Draw a level line at 1180 from V6 to the kitchen wall.
  2. Screw a bracket on V6's right face, top on the line.
  3. Hold a bracket on the kitchen wall, top on the line, and mark its holes. Drill 6 mm, plug, and screw it on with 5×50.
  4. Drop the header H onto both brackets. Screw up into it.
  5. Stand V7 and V8 on the header with brackets at foot and top, like Joint 1 and 2. V8 gets them on its left face only, then 2 plugs into the kitchen wall.
Good to know

Tips

  • Bit: use the right driver bit for the anchor screws, and press hard. A slipping bit ruins the screw head.
  • Speed: low gear and slow at the end. Stop as soon as the head sits on the steel; overdriving strips the wood.
  • Marking: mark bracket heights with a square across the face. A pencil line on both studs plus the waterpas across is quicker than measuring each one.
  • OSB later: if an OSB screw hits a bracket screw inside the stud, move it 3 cm up or down.
  • Insulation: cut the glass wool around the brackets. A small slit lets it slide over the leg.
